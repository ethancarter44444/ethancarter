Why Can You See the Moon During the Day?

Many people associate the Moon with nighttime.

But look up during the afternoon and you can often spot it clearly in the blue sky.

It may seem strange at first. If the Sun is shining brightly, shouldn't the Moon disappear?

Not at all.

The Moon is visible during the day for the same basic reason it is visible at night: it reflects sunlight toward Earth.

The Moon Doesn't Produce Its Own Light

Unlike the Sun, the Moon isn't a star and doesn't generate visible light of its own.

The bright Moon we see is sunlight reflected from its rocky surface.

Because the Moon is relatively close to Earth and reflects a significant amount of sunlight, it can remain visible even when the sky is bright.

The daytime sky is not dark enough to hide it completely.

The Moon Is Above the Horizon During the Day

The Moon is constantly moving around Earth.

As it orbits our planet, its position relative to the Sun and Earth changes.

Depending on where it is in its orbit, the Moon can be above the horizon during the morning, afternoon, evening or night.

That's why you won't see it at the same time every day.

Its rising and setting times shift continuously.

The Moon's Phase Determines When You See It

The phase of the Moon makes a big difference.

During a full Moon, the Moon is opposite the Sun in Earth's sky. It generally rises around sunset and sets around sunrise, making it primarily a nighttime object.

A new Moon, meanwhile, is positioned close to the Sun in the sky. Its illuminated side faces away from Earth, making it extremely difficult to see.

Between these extremes are phases such as the crescent and quarter Moon.

These phases can make excellent daytime viewing targets.

Why Is the Moon Sometimes Easy to See?

The Moon can be surprisingly bright compared with most objects in the daytime sky.

Against a clear blue background, its bright surface can stand out clearly.

However, visibility depends on several factors, including its phase, position in the sky, atmospheric conditions and how close it is to the horizon.

A thin crescent may be much harder to spot than a bright quarter or gibbous Moon.

Why Doesn't the Blue Sky Hide It?

Earth's atmosphere scatters sunlight in all directions.

This scattered light makes the sky appear blue and creates a bright background.

But the Moon can still appear brighter than that background.

Your eyes can therefore distinguish the Moon as a separate object.

It's similar to seeing a bright white object against a bright but less intense background.

Can You See Stars During the Day?

A few stars can technically be detected during daylight with specialised equipment, but most are far too faint for the unaided eye.

The Moon is different because it is extremely close to Earth and strongly illuminated by the Sun.

Its apparent brightness is therefore vastly greater than that of distant stars.

The Moon Is Always Moving

One of the easiest ways to understand daytime Moon sightings is to stop thinking of the Moon as a nighttime object.

It isn't.

The Moon is always somewhere in Earth's sky, although it isn't always above your horizon or positioned where you can easily see it.

Its orbit simply determines when and where it appears.

A Familiar Object With a Strange Schedule

The next time you spot the Moon in a bright afternoon sky, there's no cosmic mystery involved.

You're simply catching Earth's natural satellite at a point in its orbit where it happens to be above your horizon during daylight.

The Moon doesn't belong to the night. We just notice it more easily there.
